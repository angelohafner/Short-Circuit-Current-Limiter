How does a short-circuit current limiter reduce fault current while keeping the normal operating voltage drop low?

This animation explains a series-reactor limiter with a thyristor-protected series capacitor (TPSC), using recorded PSCAD/EMTDC simulation results.

During normal operation, the capacitor compensates the reactor's reactance. When a fault occurs, the thyristor bypass removes this compensation, allowing the reactor to limit the fault-current contribution from Source 1. The reactor remains connected throughout the sequence.

In the simulated 138 kV, 60 Hz two-source network, total symmetrical RMS fault current decreases from 79.96 kA to 49.91 kA — a 37.58% reduction. Source 2 remains directly connected to the downstream bus. These values describe the simulated case and are not instantaneous peak currents.

The video also shows fault detection, current transfer to the bypass, natural thyristor turn-off, feeder reclosure, and how reactor sizing and capacitor tuning affect operation.
